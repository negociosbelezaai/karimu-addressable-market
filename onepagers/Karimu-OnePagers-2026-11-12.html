Where Karimu should expand beyond Tanzania

About this analysis. Karimu lifts people out of extreme poverty by saturating one rural community at a time (school, clean water, health post, agriculture, savings groups) in partnership with local leadership. Today it works in 3 wards of Tanzania. At the 3 Nov 2026 strategy meeting the team set a goal of reaching 10 million people in 10 years, across 3 countries on at least 2 continents, funded by roughly $10M from large (mostly European) foundations. This report ranks 14 candidate countries (plus Tanzania as the reference) to inform that choice, which the group finalizes at the April planning session. Each country is placed by how strongly it pulls the strategy (Strategic appeal) against how realistic it is to execute there (Feasibility).

How to read the axes

Strategic appeal = ½ Impact + ½ Donor appeal

Impact blends the size of the rural-poor base, the depth of need (stunting) and the service void (rural electricity). Donor appeal is how well the country attracts international foundation money, the lens Karimu's leadership uses to raise the $10M.

Feasibility = civic space + governance + replicability + founder presence

How realistic it is to run the model there. The ★ (Indonesia, Brazil) adds the founder's local network.

What the chart says

Madagascar has the highest appeal (3rd-largest market, extreme need, very high visibility) but the lowest feasibility (weak governance, repressed civic space). Malawi is the best appeal/feasibility balance. Uganda: high appeal, governance friction. Kenya: top replicability (Swahili + English, like Tanzania). Ghana / Senegal / Côte d'Ivoire: the more feasible West-African entry, lower appeal. Indonesia (★) has mid scores, but a founder's presence and the two-continent rule make it the natural Asia anchor. Brazil: lowest appeal, global foundations unlikely to fund.
